Gravitation from Hilbert-Space Granularity:
The Sphere, the String, and the Ring

Abstract

Ever since general relativity and quantum mechanics were discovered over a century ago, physics has been on the quest for a theory that unites them. We argue that the answer has been staring us in the face: gravitation is what a granular quantum mechanics looks like at every scale. Tim Palmer’s Rational Quantum Mechanics holds that Hilbert space is granular, with a finite parameter L, which he attributes to gravity. We reverse the claim. L comes first, one universal integer, and we start from two postulates: every qubit is a string of bits on a ring of cells, no ring longer than L, and any two cells not opposite lie on one such ring. The postulates count the cosmological horizon, L2/4 rings, and one string per ring is its entropy. Jacobson’s argument then returns Einstein’s equations with G=c3⁢ℓc2/π⁢ℏ, ℓc the cell, so the measured G fixes the cell at π Planck lengths. Newton’s law is a share of energy per string; rotation curves flatten, without dark matter, where the entropy a mass removes falls below what a sphere’s strings hold inside it, at π⁢c2/12⁢RΛ=1.4×10−10 m s-2 against 1.2 observed; the cosmological constant is the closure of the ring, G⁢Λ=12⁢π⁢c3/ℏ⁢L2. Pinned from Λ and from galaxies, L=3.6×1061 and 4.3×1061, agreeing to eighteen percent. The third place where L appears on its own is the quantum computer, which will report within the decade. We predict what it will find: a ceiling of 204 entangled qubits, the same for every technology.

I Introduction

Rational Quantum Mechanics (RaQM) [1, 4, 5, 6] leaves the Schrödinger equation untouched and instead restricts the bases in which a quantum state is defined: a qubit

|ψ⟩=cos⁡θ2⁢|1⟩+ei⁢ϕ⁢sin⁡θ2⁢|−1⟩ (1)

with θ and ϕ the colatitude and azimuth of the state on the Bloch sphere, exists only in bases with

cos2⁡θ2=mL,ϕ2⁢π=nL, (2)

with m∈{0,…,L} and n∈{0,…,L−1}. The state is then a length-L bit string whose fraction of +1 entries is cos2⁡(θ/2) and whose cyclic shift by n places is the phase [4]. Born’s rule becomes a matter of counting. Bell’s inequality is evaded because, by Niven’s theorem [8, 9], the counterfactual measurement settings its derivation requires are not permitted bases [7, 5]. And because every probability is a frequency over L bits, a state spread across all 2N outcomes of N qubits needs 2N≤L, so there is a qubit capacity

Nmax=⌊log2⁡L⌋, (3)

beyond which quantum algorithms that spread their state across all of Hilbert space lose their exponential advantage [4, 2].

Palmer fixes L from gravity. State reduction is a chaotic shift map that lowers L by one each Planck time tP (in Palmer’s ordering, tP=ℏ⁢G/c5), so the reduction time is τ∗=(L−1)⁢tP; equating this to the Diósi–Penrose collapse time ℏ/EG [10, 11] gives

L=⌈EPEG⌉, (4)

where EP=ℏ/tP is the Planck energy, EG is the gravitational self-energy of the qubit’s superposition, and ⌈x⌉ is the smallest integer not less than x. Both EP and EG contain G. Gravity is the input and L the output, and L varies with the qubit, from 1064 for a quantum-dot electron to 10109 for a hyperfine ion-trap qubit [4]. Palmer closes by noting that his Nmax lands beside Davies’ cosmological information bound [12, 13] and that this is unlikely to be a coincidence.

We take that remark literally and reverse the causality. L does not exist because of gravitation. Gravitation exists thanks to L, which is a central universal constant of nature. Table Gravitation from Hilbert-Space Granularity: The Sphere, the String, and the Ring shows what the reversal yields, with numbers on both sides; the sections below derive each row. The reversal is made concrete by our companion register paper [2], which puts Palmer’s bit string on a ring of cells and derives the rest of quantum mechanics from two postulates about cells and rings, and by Ref. [3], which derives from them the three dimensions of space and the sphere the largest rings make about every observer. Here we take those postulates, identify that sphere with the observer’s cosmological horizon, which fixes L, and derive gravity. The postulates count the horizon, and the count is its entropy. From the count and the thermodynamics of horizons follow Einstein’s equations with Newton’s constant written in the cell, so that the measured G fixes the cell and the Planck length is derived; Newton’s law as a share of energy per string; the acceleration below which rotation curves flatten, with its coefficient; and the cosmological constant. At each scale one structure of the register does the work: a sphere of strings, a string running to the horizon, and the closed ring. Such a claim is tested first of all by consistency, and then by prediction. Known gravitational phenomena on every scale, each used on its own to pin L, must return the same number. Section II states the postulates, the placement, and the counts. Section III derives G from the count and fixes the cell. Section IV works through the scales in turn, from quantum to cosmological, deriving the familiar phenomena on each and recording the value of L each one pins. Section V asks whether L holds. Section VI lists what would falsify the picture.

II Postulates, Placement, and the Count of the Horizon

The postulates are those of Ref. [2], with one change of wording explained below. A cell is a place on a ring; a ring is a closed line of translation; a tick is the time light takes to cross a cell.

Postulate 1 (the register). Quantum mechanics is granular. Every qubit is a string of bits, one on each cell of a ring, each cell one length ℓc long, which light crosses in one tick. No ring has more than L cells, with L∈ℕ. The bits are the possible outcomes of one measurement of the qubit, and the string’s cyclic position on its ring is the qubit’s phase. Any two qubits of L bits can be read jointly, at one cell. The shift moves a bit to the next cell, a translation by one cell. A bit, and the measurement it answers, are carried from tick to tick. The global phase of a state is hidden: no law depends on it.

Postulate 2 (the rings). Every cell has one opposite, the cell halfway around every ring of L cells through it. Any two cells not opposite lie on one ring of L cells.

Reference [2] states the cell as one Planck length long. We write its length as ℓc and do not assume its value, because Sec. III fixes it from Newton’s constant: the cell is π Planck lengths. Everything the register derives from the postulates carries over with ℓc for ℓP, and four of its results are used here. Every probability is a frequency over the L bits, so Eq. (2) holds and Eq. (3) holds as a ceiling. A ring of L′ cells has the lap time L′⁢tc, and the least energy a massless mode on it can carry is h/L′⁢tc, the quantum of the ring. The one freedom the tick has is to reverse a bit’s sense of motion, and a particle’s mass is the rate at which it does so, α=M/mc per tick, with mc=ℏ/c⁢ℓc the mass of the cell. And space has three dimensions, so that every sphere is a two-sphere, and the directions at a cell number L, one for each cell of the ring of its quarter-lap cells, so that angles come in steps of 2⁢π/L. We write tc=ℓc/c, Ec=ℏ⁢c/ℓc, and ac=c2/ℓc for the cell’s tick, energy, and acceleration.

The placement. The sphere the largest rings make is the cosmological horizon:

RΛ=L⁢ℓc2⁢π. (5)

The sphere itself is derived: with three dimensions, the largest rings make a two-sphere about every observer, whose great circles they are, of radius L⁢ℓc/2⁢π [3]. The cosmological horizon of an observer at rest is likewise a sphere about him every great circle of which has the length 2⁢π⁢RΛ [32], and no structure of the register is larger than its sphere of largest rings; the placement identifies the two, and it is what gives L its value. Reference [2] takes it over. Palmer’s phase operator ζ is a cyclic permutation of the L-bit string, each application a rotation by exactly 2⁢π/L about the polar axis [4], so a full turn of phase counts L steps; that those steps are cells on a great circle of the horizon is not in his construction. The placement refers to the cosmological horizon without saying which one. Nothing in it fixes the radius in time; but L is a constant, so ℓc, and with it G by Sec. III, scale with the radius, and the observed constancy of G requires the radius to be constant too. In an expanding universe the only constant horizon is the de Sitter one, whose radius is set by the cosmological constant; the alternative, a horizon that grows with the Hubble radius, is excluded in Sec. IV.4 on observational grounds. Because every observer’s de Sitter horizon has the same radius, L is observer-independent, as a constant of nature must be. In the continuum that interpolates the cells, an observer’s horizon lies at the proper distance π⁢RΛ/2=L⁢ℓc/4, a quarter lap: along every largest ring through his cell it is L/4 cells on, halfway to the opposite cell of Postulate 2, and the sphere of those quarter-lap cells is the horizon the placement counts.

The reversal. Keep Palmer’s picture of state reduction as a shift map at one step per tick, so that τ∗=L⁢tc, but with L universal. With L a constant of nature the map cannot lower it; what the reversal keeps is its clock, L ticks. Then, by Eq. (5),

τ∗=L⁢tc=2⁢π⁢RΛc, (6)

the de Sitter period 2⁢π/H∞, where H∞=c/RΛ is the asymptotic Hubble rate: the time for one lap of the horizon at the speed of light. The universal energy that replaces EG is then

EcL=ℏτ∗=ℏ⁢c2⁢π⁢RΛ=kB⁢TdS, (7)

the Gibbons–Hawking temperature of the horizon [32]. Palmer’s Eq. (4), read backwards, says that gravity’s characteristic energy in the reduction process is the thermal energy of the cosmological horizon, and that every qubit shares it. Three consequences follow at once. First, τ∗∼1011 yr, so an isolated qubit never completes reduction; Palmer makes the same observation. Second, L no longer depends on the qubit, so Nmax is the same for every technology, which is the sharpest experimental difference between the two causal orderings. Third, the reversal discards mass-dependent gravitational collapse, which Palmer’s program takes to be gravity’s role in quantum mechanics [14, 15]. In Palmer’s ordering a heavy superposition has small L and reduces in milliseconds; here every system shares the 1011-yr clock, so the definiteness of macroscopic bodies is not supplied by gravity and must come, as in standard quantum theory, from environmental decoherence. The reversed theory therefore predicts no mass-dependent collapse signal of Diósi–Penrose type, consistent with the null result of the underground search of Ref. [16]; Palmer’s shift map is non-dissipative by construction and equally consistent with it, so the null result does not discriminate between the orderings. Nmax does.

The count. Postulate 2 counts the horizon, and the count is the new input to gravity. Every largest ring through a cell P passes through its opposite P′, and two rings through P share only P and P′, since two cells not opposite fix one ring; so the rings through P cut the remaining cells into disjoint sets of L−2. Any two largest rings of the horizon share a cell, since the qubits they carry can be read jointly at one cell (Postulate 1), and with it its opposite. Take a largest ring E not through P. Each ring through P meets E in one opposite pair, distinct rings in distinct pairs, and every pair of E lies on a ring through P; E has L/2 pairs, so ρ=L/2 rings pass through every cell. Every cell not opposite P lies on one of them, so the horizon has

C=2+ρ⁢(L−2)=L22−L+2⁢cells,R=C⁢ρL=L24−L2+1⁢rings, (8)

and C=2⁢R: one ring for every opposite pair of cells. Taking a cell with its opposite as a point and a largest ring as a line, the horizon is a finite projective plane of order L/2−1, with R points and R lines. That constrains L: every known plane has prime-power order, and orders 1 or 2 modulo 4 that are not sums of two squares do not exist [47]. A cell’s share of the horizon’s area is Ahor/C=2⁢ℓc2/π, and a ring’s is 4⁢ℓc2/π.

The entropy. The entropy of a horizon counts the strings its description ranges over [2], and the description of the horizon ranges over one string for each largest ring: the sense of motion along it, its doublet, which is the one qubit a ring carries by Postulate 1 whatever else is written on it. So the horizon’s entropy is its number of largest rings,

ShorkB=R=L24 (9)

to leading order. This is the register’s count, and nothing of Bekenstein and Hawking is used in it; Sec. III shows that their quarter per Planck area comes out.

Counting in the bulk. The register counts exactly on the horizon. A sphere of radius r about a body is a sphere of rings of L′=L⁢r/RΛ cells with the same structure, so it holds the fraction (r/RΛ)2 of the horizon’s cells and rings, its area in units of a cell’s share. With n=r/ℓc the distance in cells,

Nc⁢(r)=L22⁢(rRΛ)2=2⁢π2⁢n2,Ns⁢(r)=Nc⁢(r)2=π2⁢n2, (10)

cells and strings. L cancels between the count and the unit, so nothing on a sphere at solar radii knows L: its counts are n2, and n2≪L2. Between cells, space is the continuum that interpolates them, on which areas, light cones, and the Raychaudhuri equation are written; the register constructs no manifold, and the interpolation supplies the areas whose ratios do the counting.

III Newton’s Constant from the Count

Before testing the hypothesis at widely separated scales, we derive G from the count of Sec. II and the thermodynamics of horizons. Three things are taken from outside the postulates, none of them a number. A horizon is thermal, in the state whose period is the lap time of its ring, and a state of period τ has temperature ℏ/τ: that is what temperature means for such a state, and it is the content of the Gibbons–Hawking and Unruh temperatures [32, 31]. The energy that crosses a horizon is heat, δ⁢Q=T⁢d⁢S, which is Jacobson’s step [20]. And the interpolation of Sec. II carries the geometry of horizons. The entropy is the register’s, Eq. (9), and that is what is new against Jacobson’s derivation, which takes the entropy density from Bekenstein and Hawking. Verlinde’s entropic argument reaches Newton’s law at the Newtonian level [17]; we do not use it, since it reads gravity as a force driven by the entropy of the screen, which is where it is contested [18, 19], and Jacobson’s argument derives no force. The elastic response of his later work [21] is a different argument, and Sec. IV.3 uses it.

III.1 The temperature of a horizon in cells

A horizon’s ring of Lh cells has the lap time Lh⁢tc, and by the thermal assumption its temperature is

kB⁢T=ℏLh⁢tc=EcLh. (11)

For the cosmological horizon Lh=L, the lap time is 2⁢π⁢RΛ/c, and Eq. (11) is Eq. (7). An observer accelerating at a has a horizon c2/a behind him, whose thermal period is 2⁢π⁢c/a, the lap time of a ring of La=2⁢π⁢c2/a⁢ℓc cells; Eq. (11) then gives kB⁢Ta=ℏ⁢a/2⁢π⁢c, Unruh’s temperature. The register supplies the quantum of every ring, h over its lap time, and the temperature is that quantum over 2⁢π. Through every point of spacetime, in every null direction, passes the horizon of such an observer, a local Rindler horizon.

III.2 Clausius relation

On every local Rindler horizon the entropy is its count of rings, one string each, so Jacobson’s balance of heat and entropy reads

δ⁢Q=Ta⁢d⁢S=ℏ⁢a2⁢π⁢c⁢d⁢R: (12)

energy crossing a horizon adds rings, 2⁢π⁢c/ℏ⁢a of them per unit of energy, and with a ring’s share of area 4⁢ℓc2/π it adds area d⁢A=(8⁢ℓc2⁢c/ℏ⁢a)⁢δ⁢Q. The Raychaudhuri equation gives the change of area in terms of the Ricci tensor, and the balance can hold in every direction at every point only if

Rμ⁢ν−12⁢R⁢gμ⁢ν+Λ⁢gμ⁢ν=2⁢π⁢kBℏ⁢c⁢η⁢Tμ⁢ν, (13)

with η the entropy per unit area and Λ an integration constant that the argument leaves free: a stress tensor proportional to the metric has no flux through a null surface and never enters Eq. (12) [49]. This is Einstein’s equation, and comparing its coefficient with 8⁢π⁢G/c4 gives G=c3⁢kB/4⁢ℏ⁢η. Nothing in this is a force. Equation (13) is a condition on the geometry, that the count of every local horizon stays in balance with the heat that crosses it; matter then moves on the geodesics of the geometry that satisfies it.

III.3 The cell from Newton’s constant

By Eqs. (8) and (9) the entropy per unit area of the horizon is η=kB⁢R/Ahor=kB⁢(L2/4)/(L2⁢ℓc2/π), so

η=π⁢kB4⁢ℓc2,G=c3⁢ℓc2π⁢ℏ=4⁢π⁢c3⁢RΛ2ℏ⁢L2. (14)

This is the result of the section. The π is the register’s, a ring’s share of area, 4⁢ℓc2/π, against the cell squared; Jacobson’s 4, from the 8⁢π of the field equations and the 2⁢π of the thermal period, cancels the 4 of that share. Newton’s constant is measured, so Eq. (14) fixes the cell,

ℓc=π⁢ℏ⁢Gc3=π⁢ℓP=2.86×10−35⁢m, (15)

with ℓP≡ℏ⁢G/c3=ℓc/π, so the Planck length is a derived quantity, the cell over π. With it the horizon’s entropy, Eq. (9), is L2/4=π⁢RΛ2/ℓc2=Ahor/4⁢ℓP2: Bekenstein and Hawking’s quarter per Planck area [33] is the register’s one string per ring, a ring’s share of the horizon being 4⁢ℓP2. Newton’s law, a=G⁢M/r2, is the weak-field limit of Eq. (13), and its content for a static sphere is Padmanabhan’s equipartition [48], which holds in every static solution of Eq. (13): the energy inside a sphere on which the acceleration is a is two quanta kB⁢Ta for each of its strings,

M⁢c2=2⁢Ns⁢(r)⁢kB⁢Ta=π⁢n2⁢ℏ⁢ac⟹aac=απ⁢n2, (16)

with α=M/mc and n=r/ℓc. So Newton’s law reads: the body’s energy is shared equally by the strings of any sphere around it; the share per string is set by the temperature of the horizon that an observer held on the sphere sees; and the share falls as the string count grows, 1/r2. A test body n cells from a mass of flip rate α accelerates at α/π⁢n2 cell accelerations. Two remarks. First, a continuum does not gravitate: without cells the entropy per area of a horizon diverges with the cutoff, η→∞, and G→0, since no finite energy changes the area of a horizon that holds infinitely many strings per unit of area. Finite cells gravitate because each ring is worth 4⁢ℓc2/π of area, and Eq. (12) is the exchange rate. Second, Eq. (14) scales as 1/L2: with L a constant of nature, G is constant exactly when the horizon radius is, which is what singled out the de Sitter horizon in Sec. II and what makes the constancy of G and of Λ one statement (Sec. VI). The numerical value of G follows from a measurement of L that does not use G, and the quantum computer’s count is that measurement (Sec. V).

IV Gravitational Phenomena Scale by Scale

On each scale one structure of the register does the work, and we record what each scale pins. At the solar scale it is the sphere of Eq. (10); at the galactic scale the string, L cells long, running from the sphere to the horizon; at the cosmological scale the closed ring.

IV.1 Quantum scale: qubits and quantum computers

Here L acts directly through Eq. (3). The bound bites only for states spread across Hilbert space, so entanglement demonstrations of the Greenberger–Horne–Zeilinger type, which occupy two amplitudes, do not test it. Random-circuit sampling does, and the 53-, 70- and 83-qubit experiments of Refs. [24, 25, 26] imply L≳277. That is far below log2⁡L∼200, so this scale does not yet pin L. Physical qubit counts are much larger, more than a thousand superconducting qubits on IBM’s Condor processor and 6,100 atoms in a single tweezer array [27], but a count of physical qubits is not a count of qubits entangled in a state that fills Hilbert space, and the bound says nothing about it. The same holds for logical qubits, of which 48 have been demonstrated on 280 atoms [28]: each is encoded in many physical qubits confined to a small, highly structured subspace, the situation Palmer already exempts when he discusses macroscopic quantum states [4]. What this scale will do, on the timescale Palmer gives for factoring tests [4, 30], is measure L directly. The test is a random circuit on N fault-tolerant logical qubits, deep enough to spread its state across the 2N outcomes, followed by its inverse. In quantum mechanics the state returns to the initial one with the gate fidelity; here it does not once N exceeds log2⁡L, because the intermediate state gives probability to more outcomes than L bits can hold. Error correction does not lift the ceiling, which is a count of outcomes and not a rate of errors. The reversed theory’s prediction on this scale is Nmax=204, the same for every technology (Sec. V).

State reduction on this scale runs on the universal clock (6) and never completes; nothing observable changes for laboratory qubits, exactly as in RaQM.

IV.2 Terrestrial and solar scales: the sphere

Section III gave Einstein’s equations with G=c3⁢ℓc2/π⁢ℏ, Eq. (14), and Newton’s law as their weak-field limit, Eq. (16). Gravitational redshift [34], the perihelion advance of Mercury, light deflection, and gravitational-wave propagation then follow as they do in general relativity. Mercury’s anomalous advance 6⁢π⁢G⁢M⊙/(p⁢c2), with p=aorb⁢(1−e2) the semi-latus rectum of the orbit, is 42.98′′ per century and the deflection of starlight at the solar limb 4⁢G⁢M⊙/(c2⁢R⊙) is 1.75′′, both as observed. At the Earth’s orbit, α=1.62×1038 and n=5.22×1045 in Eq. (16) give 5.93×10−3 m s-2. They depend on L and RΛ only through G, i.e., only through the cell ℓc=2⁢π⁢RΛ/L.

What this scale pins is therefore ℓc, the combination 2⁢π⁢RΛ/L, and not L on its own. The count of a sphere is n2, with L cancelled against the unit, Eq. (10); and the horizon of an observer held at solar radii is his own, La≪L, since a≫c2/RΛ there. This is as it should be, since solar-system gravity is described by general relativity to exquisite precision, and a theory in which L showed up there separately from RΛ would be wrong. The situation is the exact gravitational analogue of Palmer’s observation that RaQM and QM are indistinguishable for small numbers of qubits: the granularity shows itself only at the extremes, at large N in the quantum case and at small acceleration in the gravitational case. To forestall a natural misreading: the direct kinematic effect of a 2⁢π/L phase grid on an orbit is ∼10−61 rad per revolution, fifty-four orders of magnitude below Mercury’s 5×10−7 rad; angular discreteness does nothing to orbits. The only route by which the horizon could enter solar-system dynamics is through the galactic acceleration of Sec. IV.3, whose corrections are of order 10−10 m s-2, eight orders of magnitude below solar gravity at Earth’s orbit; planetary ephemerides are only now reaching that sensitivity [45]. The push of Sec. IV.4 is smaller still, 5×10−25 m s-2 at the Earth’s orbit.

IV.3 Galactic scale: the string

At the scales above, L and RΛ entered gravity only through their ratio, and two facts of the register did no work. A string has L bits, one on each cell of a largest ring, which reaches the horizon: the strings a sphere counts are not confined to it. And no ring has more than L cells, so no horizon is colder than Ec/L: the ring La of a held observer cannot exceed L, which by Eq. (11) means a≥c2/RΛ. Both enter at accelerations of order the horizon’s surface gravity, c2/RΛ=c⁢H∞=5.4×10−10 m s-2. That the scale at which rotation curves flatten is the horizon’s acceleration, up to a coefficient, is not ours: Milgrom reached it from the Unruh temperature of de Sitter space [37], and Verlinde from its entanglement entropy [21]. What is ours is the count that fixes where L enters and the coefficient, which we now derive, following Verlinde’s elastic argument with the register’s counts in place of his entropy assignments.

A mass draws the horizon in. Einstein’s equations hold from Sec. III on. Their solution for a mass M inside the cosmological horizon is Schwarzschild–de Sitter, whose outer horizon lies at RΛ−G⁢M/c2 to first order in M. By Eq. (15), G⁢M/c2=ℓc⁢α/π: the mass draws the horizon in by α/π cells. Its circumference loses 2⁢α cells, and since the entropy is the square of the circumference in cells over four, Eq. (9), the loss is (L/2)⋅2⁢α,

SM=L⁢α=M⁢c2kB⁢TdS=2⁢π⁢M⁢c⁢RΛℏ, (17)

Verlinde’s Eq. (25) in cells [21]. The entropy a mass takes from the horizon is its flip rate times the length of the ring, its energy over the horizon’s temperature, and it does not depend on the cell’s size. For the Sun L⁢α=5.9×1099; for a galaxy of 1011⁢M⊙, 5.9×10110. Within a sphere of radius r≪RΛ the mass removes SM⁢(r)=2⁢π⁢M⁢c⁢r/ℏ=2⁢π⁢n⁢α, Verlinde’s Eq. (28): the sphere at geodesic distance r from the mass loses the area 8⁢π⁢G⁢M⁢r/c2, which at a ring’s share 4⁢ℓc2/π is 2⁢π⁢n⁢α rings.

Where the claim is carried. The sphere at r counts Ns⁢(r)=π2⁢n2 strings, Eq. (10), the horizon’s L2/4 scaled by the ratio of areas, but its strings are not confined to it. A string on the sphere runs along a largest ring to the horizon, a quarter lap from the body, L/4 cells on along every largest ring through its cell (Sec. II). The sense of motion that is the string’s entropy is carried by every bit of it alike (Postulate 1), so the string’s one unit is spread evenly over its cells, and of the L/4 between the body and the horizon, n lie inside the sphere. The entropy the sphere’s strings hold inside it is therefore the fraction n/(L/4)=4⁢n/L of their count,

Sin⁢(r)=4⁢nL⁢Ns⁢(r)=4⁢π2⁢n3L, (18)

a volume law. Verlinde postulates a volume law for the entanglement entropy of de Sitter space, the horizon entropy divided evenly over the bulk, and normalizes it on a flat ball of radius RΛ, which gives the fraction 2⁢π⁢n/L; his Eq. (81) says that each cell of the sphere contributes the fraction given by the ratio of the proper distances from the center to that cell and to the horizon, and in the register that ratio is a count, n over L/4. While the mass removes more entropy within the sphere than its strings hold there, SM⁢(r)>Sin⁢(r), the sphere is saturated: what fixes the share is the two-dimensional count of Eq. (10), and Newton holds. The crossing is where the two are equal,

SM(r)=Sin(r):Ns=π2Lα,gN=c2π⁢RΛ, (19)

so rotation curves depart from Newton at an acceleration of order the horizon’s, with the coefficient fixed by the count.

The elastic response. Below the crossing the entropy inside the sphere exceeds what the mass removes, and the remainder responds as an incompressible elastic medium: the removed entropy occupies the volume VM=SM⁢(r)⁢V0, with V0=V⁢(r)/Sin⁢(r)=L⁢ℓc3/3⁢π the volume per unit of entropy from Eq. (18); the strain ε outside the removed region obeys ∫0rε2⁢A⁢𝑑r′≤VM⁢(r), with equality under Verlinde’s assumption on the principal strain, his Eqs. (44)–(46) and (88); and the strain is the apparent surface mass density, ΣD=(aΛ/8⁢π⁢G)⁢ε with aΛ=c2/RΛ, his Eq. (43). The energy of a strain is quadratic in it, which is why the apparent mass goes as the square root of the removed entropy rather than in proportion to it. Differentiating with M fixed, ε2=(1/A)⁢d⁢VM/d⁢r, and with Gauss’s law Σ=g/4⁢π⁢G,

gD2=aM⁢gN,aM=π12⁢c2RΛ=1.42×10−10⁢m⁢s−2, (20)

which is (π2/6)⁢c2/L⁢ℓc in the cell. Equation (20) is an upper bound, saturated under the assumption above. The apparent dark mass is MD=M⁢n⁢π3/6⁢L⁢α: it grows by a fixed amount per cell of distance, as a string does, and that is the geometry of this scale. Verlinde’s own normalization gives aM=c2/6⁢RΛ=0.90×10−10 m s-2, his Eq. (102); the register’s count is π/2 larger. The total field is g=gN+gD, and in the deep regime, where gD≫gN,

g=aM⁢gN,vc4=aM⁢G⁢M, (21)

flat rotation curves and the baryonic Tully–Fisher relation, with M the baryonic mass alone. This is Milgrom’s phenomenology [35], confirmed as a tight empirical law across galaxy types by the radial acceleration relation [40], with one a0 for galaxies whose stellar masses span five orders of magnitude. The measured scale is a0=(1.20±0.02±0.24)×10−10 m s-2 [40], and (1.19±0.04±0.09)×10−10 with a tighter mass-to-light calibration [50]. Equation (20) lies 0.9⁢σ above the first and 2.3⁢σ above the second: observation saturates 85 percent of the bound. With Verlinde’s normalization the observed a0 exceeds the bound, which is the known underprediction of his estimate [29]. For attribution, the coefficients in units of c⁢H∞ are: 1, Milgrom’s vacuum scale [37]; 2, the de Sitter temperature in an equipartition [36]; 1/6=0.17, Verlinde [21]; 0.13 after the correction of Refs. [23, 22]; π/12=0.26, this paper; against 0.22 observed. The Tully–Fisher speed of a 1011⁢M⊙ galaxy is 208 km s-1 by Eq. (21), against 200 observed. Verlinde’s specific interpolation between the Newtonian and deep regimes shows tension with the radial acceleration relation in the transition region [29]; what pins L here is the deep-regime scaling (21), which survives that test, and the transition function remains open.

Why the temperature cannot lead. The floor Ec/L could be taken as the mechanism instead of the bits. In de Sitter space a detector accelerating at a sees the two horizons combined in quadrature, kB⁢T=(ℏ/2⁢π⁢c)⁢a2+aΛ2 [51], and if gravity followed the excess of that temperature over the floor, as Milgrom proposed [37], Eq. (16) would give a=gN2+2⁢aΛ⁢gN: the right deep form with aM=2⁢c2/RΛ, nine times the observed value, and a transition 38 percent above Newton at gN=10⁢a0 where the observed relation is 4 percent above. The floor marks the scale; the law is in the strings.

To be explicit: in this framework there is no dark-matter halo, nor the smearing of energy-momentum over neighboring space-times by which Invariant Set Theory reaches the same phenomena with Λ=0 [14, 15]. Galactic rotation is set by the baryons, G, and the horizon radius, which Λ fixes. The known difficulties of any such account, galaxy clusters [41] and the acoustic peaks of the microwave background [44], are not addressed here and remain open.

This scale pins L. The deep-regime law is observation, now extended by weak lensing to circular velocities that stay flat out to a megaparsec, on the same baryonic Tully–Fisher relation [39]. Setting Eq. (20) equal to the measured a0,

Lgal=π26⁢c2a0⁢ℓc=4.3×1061. (22)

Since Eq. (20) is a bound, so is this: L≤4.3×1061, with equality where the bound is saturated, and the cosmological value of Sec. IV.4 lies inside it, at 85 percent.

IV.4 Cosmological scale: the ring

The placement introduced a horizon radius RΛ; the universe supplies one. The observed cosmological constant Λ=1.09×10−52 m-2 [44] corresponds to a de Sitter radius RΛ=3/Λ=1.66×1026 m [32], and Eq. (5) with the cell of Eq. (15) gives

Lcos=2⁢π⁢RΛℓc=3.6×1061. (23)

Three inputs only: the measured Λ, de Sitter geometry for RΛ, and the cell. Read together with Eq. (14), this says that Newton’s constant and the cosmological constant are not independent of each other:

G⁢Λ=12⁢π⁢c3ℏ⁢L2, (24)

using Λ=3/RΛ2. The strength of gravity and the acceleration of the universe are a single constant, with L as the exchange rate between them.

The structure at this scale is the closed ring: L cells, a fixed number, and no ring of the register is larger. The horizon is L2/4 of them, Eq. (9), one string each at the temperature Ec/L, Eq. (7), so its energy, entropy times temperature, is

EΛ=L24⁢EcL=L⁢Ec4=c4⁢RΛ2⁢G=1.0×1070⁢J, (25)

one cell energy for every four cells of the ring, and the last equality is Eq. (14). Spread over the interior it is ρΛ=3⁢π2⁢ρc/2⁢L2=5.3×10−10 J m-3, with ρc=Ec/ℓc3: one factor of L because the count is of a sphere and not of a volume, one because a string’s energy is Ec/L, which is how Ref. [49] closes the 123 orders of magnitude of the vacuum energy. In Eq. (13) Λ is an integration constant, and the largest ring is the boundary condition that fixes it. The energy of Eq. (25) is that of a constant density with pressure −ρΛ, which is what it means for it to have no flux through null surfaces, and the active gravitational mass of such a density, ρ+3⁢p=−2⁢ρΛ, is negative. In the weak field about a mass M the solution of Eq. (13) is Schwarzschild–de Sitter, and its acceleration toward the mass is

g⁢(r)=G⁢Mr2−c2⁢rRΛ2,aac=απ⁢n2−4⁢π2⁢nL2. (26)

The second term is the push: the horizon’s surface gravity scaled by r/RΛ. It grows with r because the ring’s content is a constant density, and a larger sphere holds more of it. Every free body recedes from every other, which is de Sitter expansion at H∞=c/RΛ=2⁢π/L⁢tc, the Hubble rate being 2⁢π over the lap time of Eq. (6). The push balances the Newtonian pull at rz3=G⁢M⁢RΛ2/c2, in cells nz3=α⁢L2/4⁢π3: 111 pc for the Sun, and 1.6 Mpc for the Local Group at 3×1012⁢M⊙, the scale of its observed zero-velocity surface. In the deep regime the pull of Eq. (21) balances the push at r2=RΛ2⁢aM⁢G⁢M/c2, which is 4 Mpc for 1011⁢M⊙: flat rotation curves end where the ring’s push overtakes the string’s pull, beyond the megaparsec to which weak lensing now finds them flat [39].

The choice of the de Sitter radius rather than the present Hubble radius c/H0 in the placement was anticipated in Sec. II; here is the observation that forces it. Were the horizon in Eq. (5) the Hubble radius with L fixed, then G∝RH2 would drift at G˙/G=2⁢H⁢(1+q)≈6×10−11 yr-1, with H the Hubble rate and q≈−0.53 the present deceleration parameter. That is six hundred times the lunar-laser-ranging bound [46]. With RΛ constant, G, aM and Λ are all constant, as Eq. (24) requires.

V Does L Hold?

Table 2 collects the results. Two scales fix L independently, one as a value and one as a bound that observation saturates to 85 percent. The galactic scale, through the acceleration at which rotation curves flatten and the coefficient of Eq. (20), gives L≤4.3×1061, with equality where the bound is saturated. The cosmological scale, through the size of the de Sitter horizon, gives 3.6×1061. They agree to eighteen percent, a quarter of a bit in log2⁡L, the scale the quantum prediction lives on: 204.7 against 204.5, and both give Nmax=204. The two measurements come from scales five orders of magnitude apart and use entirely different methods. Their agreement does not test the exponent, which both take from the cell; ℓc cancels in the ratio Lgal/Lcos=π⁢c2/12⁢a0⁢RΛ. What it tests is the coefficient: that c2/a0 is 12⁢RΛ/π, which is the count of Sec. IV.3.

We do not pretend that the scale is new. It is the coincidence a0∼c⁢H0 that Milgrom noted in 1983 [35] and has since written as 2⁢π⁢a0≈c⁢H0≈c2⁢Λ/3, leaving open which of the two a0 is tied to [38]; that the scale is the horizon’s acceleration is emergent gravity’s explanation, not ours. What the framework adds is fourfold. It counts the coefficient, to within the fifteen percent by which observation falls short of the bound. It ties a0 to RΛ rather than to H⁢(z), so the scale does not evolve. It turns the coincidence into a fixed relation, Eq. (24) with Eq. (20), Λ=432⁢aM2/π2⁢c4. And it ties the same L to a third quantity that has nothing to do with either galaxies or cosmology: the number of qubits a quantum computer can usefully entangle. That third pin is where the theory is falsifiable.

The terrestrial and solar scales do not pin L, and we have said why. They pin ℓc, the combination 2⁢π⁢RΛ/L. For the reversed theory they establish that gravity exists at all: a horizon whose entropy is its count of rings produces Newton’s law and Einstein’s equations with the observed strength, once the cell is π Planck lengths. This fixes the cell (Sec. III); it is not a check on L, and we do not count it as evidence for L.

The quantum scale will pin L directly. The bound 2N≤L gives Nmax=204 at both values of L (Appendix A). The bound is a ceiling that any representation must respect and not a construction that attains it, and Ref. [2] shows it is soft by a unit or two when the deepest conditionals of a state must be told apart to more than two cells; Palmer’s bit count 2N+1−2≤N⁢L, which gives 211, is the weaker condition, since it counts bits where the constraint is outcomes [6, 2]. Neither touches the point that matters, which is that the ceiling is universal. A measurement of Nmax near 200 for one technology and near 400 for another would restore Palmer’s causal order; the same value for all would confirm ours.

That measurement also closes the loop in the direction the title promises. With L known, Eq. (5) gives the cell from the size of the universe, and Eq. (14) gives Newton’s constant. Gravity’s strength is then a derived quantity: the number of rings on the horizon. Converting a measured Nmax into L uses the bound directly, 2Nmax≤L<2Nmax+1, a factor of two; hence ℓc follows to a factor two and G to a factor four. A ceiling of 204 brackets G between 3.3×10−11 and 1.3×10−10 m3 kg-1 s-2, and the measured value lies at the middle of the bracket, log2⁡L=204.5; a ceiling of 205 would put G at a quarter of the measured value, and 203 at four times it if the bound is tight. Nothing else in the theory can do this: in Eq. (20) only the product L⁢ℓc=2⁢π⁢RΛ appears, so galactic and cosmological data fix each other and leave ℓc untouched. Only the quantum computer separates L from ℓc.

Two further remarks. The universal L found here, ∼4×1061, lies below every per-qubit value Palmer obtains from Eq. (4), so if both mechanisms were at work, the cosmological one would be the binding constraint. And Davies’ bound [12], which Palmer flagged as suspiciously close to his own numbers, is explained rather than merely matched. The horizon has L2/2=6.6×10122 cells, which is Lloyd’s estimate of 10122 bits for the observable universe [13], and Davies’ N=log2⁡10122≈405 is their logarithm, 2⁢log2⁡L−1=408, to the precision of Lloyd’s estimate. Palmer’s capacity bound counts the bit-string length L, not the cells of the horizon, so Nmax=log2⁡L≈204, half Davies’ exponent, and the factor of two now has a definite origin.

VI Predictions and Falsification

  1. 1.

    A universal entanglement ceiling of 204 qubits, the same for photonic, trapped-ion, superconducting and semiconductor qubits, insensitive to isolation and to logical error correction. The test is the random circuit and its inverse of Sec. IV. A return with the gate fidelity at well over 205 logical qubits falsifies the ceiling; technology dependence of a ceiling falsifies the reversal and restores Eq. (4).

  2. 2.

    Newton’s constant from the ceiling. G=4⁢π⁢c3⁢RΛ2/ℏ⁢L2, Eq. (14), with 2Nmax≤L<2Nmax+1: a measured ceiling returns G to a factor of four, and 204 returns the measured value at the middle of its bracket. A ceiling of 205 falsifies Eq. (14); one of 203 does so unless the unit or two by which the bound is soft (Sec. V) accounts for it.

  3. 3.

    Flat rotation curves from baryons alone, with vc4=aM⁢G⁢Mbaryon, Eq. (21), and aM=π⁢c2/12⁢RΛ=1.4×10−10 m s-2, Eq. (20), an upper bound of which observation saturates 85 percent. A measured a0 above 1.4×10−10 m s-2, robust detection of a dark-matter particle, or a galaxy whose rotation curve requires one, falsifies the galactic sector. That loses the second pin of L; the horizon pin, Eq. (23), the relation (24), and items 1 and 2 do not use it.

  4. 4.

    No redshift evolution of a𝟎. The acceleration scale is tied to the constant RΛ of the placement, not to H⁢(z); proposals with a0∝c⁢H⁢(z) predict a scale 1.8 times larger at redshift z=1, and this framework predicts none.

  5. 5.

    Constancy of 𝚲. With L fixed, Eq. (14) gives G∝RΛ2, so the lunar-laser-ranging bound on G˙/G [46] requires the horizon radius, and with it Λ, to be constant to one part in 1013 per year, and their product G⁢Λ=12⁢π⁢c3/ℏ⁢L2, Eq. (24), is fixed by L alone. Since a dark-energy component with equation of state w evolves as ρ˙/ρ=−3⁢H⁢(1+w), this is a bound on the present-day equation of state, |1+w0|≲5×10−4 (Appendix A). It applies to the present epoch, the only one lunar laser ranging probes. DESI DR2 prefers evolving dark energy over a constant Λ at 3.1⁢σ with the cosmic microwave background, and at 2.8⁢σ to 4.2⁢σ with supernovae added, depending on the sample [42]; the Lyman-α analysis of the same release gives 2.7⁢σ with the microwave background and 3.2⁢σ with supernovae added [43]. A confirmed evolution is incompatible with the framework as stated. Here w=−1 is not assumed, as it is in ΛCDM, but follows from L and ℓc being constant, so the framework predicts that the preference will not survive.

VII Conclusion

Palmer built a discrete quantum mechanics and used gravity to set its granularity. We have argued that the granularity comes first. Two postulates about cells and rings, the sphere they make read as the cosmological horizon, and the thermodynamics of horizons are enough to count the horizon, to make its entropy the count of its rings, to obtain general relativity through Jacobson’s argument with Newton’s constant written in the cell, to derive the Planck length as the cell over π and Bekenstein and Hawking’s quarter with it, and to bind Newton’s constant and the cosmological constant into one. With the elastic response of the medium added, they produce flat galactic rotation curves with no dark matter at an acceleration whose coefficient is counted. At each scale one structure of the register does the work: a sphere of strings, whose count dilutes a share of energy as the inverse square; a string running to the horizon, which carries a mass’s claim in proportion to the distance; and the closed ring, whose fixed content pushes. Palmer’s collapse formula inverts into a universal reduction clock equal to the de Sitter period, with the horizon temperature as the energy scale, and mass-dependent gravitational collapse drops out of the theory. The check is whether the two scales on which L appears on its own in gravity agree. They do, to eighteen percent, and the coefficient of the galactic scale is now a number the register counts rather than a coincidence it inherits. The test is the third scale on which L appears on its own, the quantum computer, and it will report within the decade.
